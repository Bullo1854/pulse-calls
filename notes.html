Sales per guest, ticket, and sales per hour

These three are pushed to the manager and the executive on their own. Rain can excuse a light door. It does not excuse a guest who left without a ticket, a ticket that shrank, or an hour that did not pay for itself.

  • Average ticket — net sales divided by tickets, versus this store’s own pace. Flagged at 15% or more under.
  • Sales per hour — net sales divided by paid hours, versus this store’s own pace. Flagged at 15% or more under.

Weather

Dates in the file are in 2029. There is no weather feed. Each store-day gets simulated weather for its market’s climate, including the comparable day last year. The same inch does not mean the same thing.

  • An inch of snow on the New England coast costs about 6% of the day. Stores and roads are built for it.
  • An inch of snow in Georgia or Alabama costs about 40% of the day. It shuts the roads.
  • An inch of rain on the Florida Gulf costs about 5%. Afternoon storms are normal.
  • An inch of rain in New England costs about 10%. A soaker is not the daily pattern.
  • A 95° day in Florida is priced in. The same day in New England is not.
