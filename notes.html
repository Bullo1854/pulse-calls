The next question is who to call.

Page 1 already answers how the company did. A market manager standing in a doorway does not need a second scoreboard. They need the three stores that are off their own pace, the reason, and the sentence to open the call with.

What the screen is for

Five seconds: the dollar hole and the names. Thirty seconds: the action on the card. A tap: the seven days against that store’s own weekday habit, and whether the hole is the door, the ticket, or the cancel book.

Scope stands in for role. Company is the exec landing. North, Central, South, and each market are the regional and market landings. A clean market says so, and points at where the calls actually are.

Data flow

The practice file is one JSON: 23 stores, 865 sleep products, 5,407 store-days, 31,591 product rows, 17 Jan 2029 through 9 Sep 2029. The phone never loads that. A build step writes a 72kb call list. The app reads that file only.

Week net on screen is the sum of net sales for 3–9 Sep, 161 rows, $1,878,375.72. That is the tie-out. Ratios are recomputed. The file’s own margin percent and close rate are wrong often enough that using them would fail the penny test the moment someone audits a card.

Where it breaks at real scale

  • Baselines belong in a nightly job. The phone should receive the call list, not eight months of ticks.
  • Peer set should be format and climate, not the whole company, once there are hundreds of stores.
  • There are no people in the file, so the call names a store, not a manager.
  • Order-level cancel reasons are missing. Harrow Point’s Thursday is −$18,821 cancelled; the sleep product file only explains about −$5k of returns. The action stops at “pull the tickets.”
  • A live 15-minute feed does not belong on this page. Mixing it with the daily close is how yesterday gets read as today.
  • Marks on this phone are not a learning system. A real one logs the call and the next week’s gap, for the region, not for one handset.
  • Promo weeks will false-flag if the baseline ignores the promo calendar.

The rule’s own track record is on the phone: 17 of 30 prior flags were still off the following week. That is useful, and it is not magic.
