The call list

A store is in the top group at 30% or more under its own pace. The dollars on the right are the pace gap. The percent under them is revenue pace. The words on the line are what went wrong: guests came and tickets did not, the ticket shrank, the door slowed, or traffic did not close. Sales per hour is not used on that list. It is still on the store card.

The net sales card is that same gap. It shows the period, then the subtraction: sales minus the 8-week pace. Fairhaven for Sep 3–9 is $33,460 minus a $77,788 pace, which is −$44.3k and −57%. A different week is a different subtraction.

The cards

Every sales and traffic card uses the same 8-week same-weekday pace as the list. A day uses that weekday. A week sums the seven days. Red is 15% or more under. Green is 5% or more ahead. Inside that band the card stays plain.

  • Net sales — the pace gap, the same dollars as the list.
  • Margin — the week rate versus the 8-week habit, after financing cost. The stored discount percent is not used.
  • Sales per guest — net sales divided by traffic.
  • Sales per hour — net sales divided by paid hours.
  • Average ticket — net sales divided by tickets.
  • Traffic — guests versus the 8-week same-weekday pace.
  • Close — tickets divided by traffic, in points versus that pace.

Weather is a potential, not an actual

The weather card does not change the sales or traffic numbers. It is a possible adjustment for a coming day, not a figure already taken out. Red means the pattern is worse than last year and could cost a day. Green means it is better and could help. The percent is what this climate could move, not what it did. There is no weather feed. The pattern is simulated for the market.

  • An inch of snow on the New England coast could cost about 6% of a coming day. Stores and roads are built for it.
  • An inch of snow in Georgia or Alabama could cost about 40% of a coming day. It shuts the roads.
  • An inch of rain on the Florida Gulf could cost about 5%. Afternoon storms are normal.
  • An inch of rain in New England could cost about 10%.
  • A 95° day in Florida is priced in. The same day in New England is not.

North is the Northeast. Central is the Carolina piedmont and Tennessee. South is Florida, Georgia, and Alabama.

Note — Easter shifts the year-ago week

The file’s last-year date is 364 days back, the same weekday. That misses Easter. Easter 2029 was Sunday, April 1. Easter 2028 was Sunday, April 16. The gap is 14 days. On the weeks ending April 1, April 8, and April 15, Page 2 shifts the comparable 14 days so the week is read against the same days relative to Easter. Easter Sunday is not a normal Sunday: the hole on April 1 is the holiday, and it is taken out before the rest of the week is coached. Last year’s sales are not in the file. The shift is the comparable, not a second set of receipts.

Note — product mix is weekly

Product variability is the written file for the week, not a day. A category is flagged when its share of that week is 15 points or more under the store’s own prior weeks. A single product is flagged when it carries 35% or more of the week and is 15 points above its habit. The product file is not the whole sales book.

What to coach

  • Guests came, tickets did not. One-up the next open. Do not buy it with a discount.
  • The ticket shrank. Step the mattress, then the base, then the care plan.
  • The door slowed. Check the circular and the road sign. Weather is a possible factor, not the excuse already applied.
  • Traffic did not close. Watch the first sit. No one works a guest alone.
  • Hours did not pay. Match posted hours to who worked. This is on the card, not the call list.
  • A visit counts the same as a call.

Actions

A day action is judged on the next same weekday. A week action is judged on the next close. Held means −12% or better versus that pace, with weather still left in the number. The log records who entered it. New entries stay on this phone.
